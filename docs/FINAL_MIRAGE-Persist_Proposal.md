# MIRAGE-Persist: A Post-Removal Identification Protocol for Persistent Policy Hysteresis in Deception-Aware AI Agents

## 1. Title
MIRAGE-Persist: Counterfactual Measurement of Persistent Policy Hysteresis in Deception-Aware AI Agents.

## 2. One-Sentence Thesis
A post-deception behavioural difference is not evidence of durable policy redirection until every recorded state carrier has been experimentally isolated through matched, budget-equalized, reset-controlled post-removal continuations.

## 3. Abstract
Autonomous and semi-autonomous cyber agents are increasingly evaluated against environmental deceptions such as decoys, honeytokens, and misleading artifacts. Existing evaluations measure effects while the deception is present: attack success, decoy engagement, delay, or resource drain. These measures cannot tell whether an agent has adopted a different policy or is merely operating from a different memory, environment, resource budget, or scaffold history. MIRAGE-Persist treats persistence as a causal estimand rather than a qualitative observation. From a reproducible checkpoint we fork a treated branch that receives a bounded deception and a matched never-treated branch. We then remove the deception through a versioned operation, equalize budgets and randomness, and selectively reset candidate state carriers (memory, external environment, resource budget, scaffold and planner history, and model instance), each with an untreated-reset control that measures reset-induced artifacts. We measure the Post-Removal Effect (PRE) between treated-then-removed and never-treated future-trajectory distributions across multiple horizons, estimate a persistence half-life, decompose the effect across carriers, and quantify recovery and benign false-suspicion cost under matched compute. The work builds on and explicitly credits recent counterfactual-replay and causal-attribution defenses (AgentSentry, AttriGuard, MELON) rather than claiming those mechanisms as new. The contribution is the post-removal identification protocol, its carrier decomposition, and a persistence half-life for tool-using agents. All experiments use local open-weight models and open-source infrastructure. The design retains scientific value under positive, mixed, or null results: an equivalence-tested null would show that widely reported persistent deception is largely an artifact of history, environment, or resource mismatch.

## 4. Executive Summary
The central question is deliberately narrow: after a temporary bounded deception is removed, does the distribution of an agent's future trajectories differ from a matched never-treated continuation once recorded carriers and costs are equalized, and if so at what horizon, through which carrier, and does any residual survive fresh model and context re-instantiation. We answer this with three tightly connected empirical contributions. First, we measure post-removal persistence with a component-wise PRE and a persistence half-life. Second, we decompose that persistence across recorded carriers using a matched reset design with untreated-reset artifact controls and a fresh-instance residual test. Third, we evaluate recovery, false suspicion, and benign utility under budget-matched compute so that any security benefit is reported against its operational cost. Cross-episode adaptation is retained as a clearly marked optional extension, and a mechanistic audit of any residual is deferred to future work because the reviewed literature shows that such claims require a reset-surviving residual to exist first and heavy validation to be credible. The scientific object, the estimand over future trajectory distributions with carrier decomposition and an equivalence-tested null, is not addressed by any of the 103 reviewed comparison reports, although several of them supply the replay, attribution, and checkpointing machinery on which the protocol is built.

## 5. Research Problem
Deception has become a practical defense against autonomous attackers, and a growing set of studies show that environmental artifacts, poisoned tool outputs, and misleading content can delay, divert, or stop an agent while the manipulation is present [4,5,7,8,31,32,33,34,35]. The dominant metrics remain immediate: attack success rate, harmful action rate, decoy contact, or utility under attack. An agent that spends twenty steps on a decoy may later resume the exact original attack, while a short interaction may permanently prune a branch from its planner. Without matched post-removal continuations these two cases are observationally confounded. The problem MIRAGE-Persist addresses is that the field lacks an identification protocol that can separate a durable change in the agent's induced policy from the mundane persistence of recorded state such as memory contents, environment mutations, depleted budgets, and accumulated scaffold history.

## 6. Motivation and Scientific Significance
Three observations motivate the work. First, immediate potency and post-removal persistence are distinct claims that require distinct evidence, and conflating them inflates the apparent durability of deception defenses. Second, recent defenses have introduced exactly the machinery needed to study the question rigorously: boundary checkpointing, cached counterfactual replay, teacher-forced shadow execution, and action-level causal attribution [1,2,3]. These make a disciplined post-removal study feasible, but they answer a different question, namely which channel drives the next action at a contaminated boundary and how to repair it. Third, one of the closest defenses reports no sustained long-horizon takeovers on a standard agent benchmark and only modest benefit from temporal aggregation [1], which raises the prior probability that strong post-removal persistence is small or rare. That possibility is not a weakness of the proposal. A carefully identified null is scientifically valuable because it would reattribute reported persistence to recorded state and would give defenders a principled reason to focus on state hygiene rather than on the agent's presumed internal objective.

## 7. Literature Synthesis
This proposal was developed against 103 structured comparison reports spanning deception defenses, prompt-injection attacks and benchmarks, agent memory and scaffolding, causal inference, cyber-agent environments, and mechanistic interpretability. The synthesis yields four consistent findings. First, the immediate-effect literature is mature: attacks and defenses are measured while the manipulation is present, and immediate success is repeatedly cautioned against as evidence of lasting change [4,27,31,32,33,34]. Second, the carrier literature establishes that recorded state can carry later behaviour: memory and lesson stores [20,21,22,23,24], scaffold and planner history [43,44,45,37], resource budgets [25], and environment reachability [26,36] all shape subsequent decisions. Third, three defenses supply the counterfactual-replay and causal-attribution building blocks that MIRAGE-Persist reuses [1,2,3]. Fourth, the mechanistic-interpretability literature offers a full toolkit for a latent audit but uniformly warns that such analyses must be conditioned on an established behavioural residual and validated on held-out interventions [58,59,60,61,62,63,64]. The evidence limitation is stated explicitly in Section 41: the reports assert that their authors read the complete papers, and this proposal relies on the reports as supplied evidence rather than on independent inspection of the original papers.

## 8. Closest Prior Work
Four papers overlap with the central scientific object, the methodology, or the intervention setting, and each is credited rather than superseded.

AgentSentry [1] is the closest methodological precursor. At each tool-return boundary it restores a fixed state and dry-runs four regimes crossing original or neutral user input with original or sanitized mediator content to estimate boundary causal effects, localizes takeover with a windowed rule, then purifies tool, retrieval, and memory content and continues. It already provides boundary checkpointing, cached counterfactual replay, temporal causal diagnostics, and purification as recovery. It differs from MIRAGE-Persist because its estimand is the next action at the contaminated boundary and its sanitized mediator is derived from the attacked content, not from a matched never-treated future. It does not fork treated-then-removed and never-treated continuations, does not equalize their budgets and histories, does not selectively reset individual carriers, and does not report a persistence half-life. It also reports no sustained long-horizon takeovers on its benchmark, which motivates equivalence testing here.

AttriGuard [2] reframes injection defense as action-level causal attribution using teacher-forced shadow replay that fixes prior actions and rewrites only observations, then tests whether a proposed call survives. It establishes synchronized counterfactual replay and action-level attribution as prior art. It performs a simultaneous verification branch rather than a temporary intervention followed by removal, and it neither estimates a continuation distribution nor resets carriers.

MELON [3] performs masking-based counterfactual re-execution and compares tool-call sequences with a utility-aware detector. It supplies counterfactual re-execution and action comparison, but it never removes the injected content before measuring behaviour, uses deterministic decoding rather than replicated stochastic continuations, and reports no post-removal effect, half-life, or carrier reset.

HoneyTrap [4] is the closest deception-as-defense predecessor. It coordinates four defender agents to mislead an attacker during exposure and reports an attacker-resource-consumption metric that deliberately rewards token drain. Because that metric is exactly the budget-depletion pathway MIRAGE-Persist must equalize before claiming persistence, HoneyTrap is a source of intervention design and a stress case for the budget-reset arm, not a predecessor of the post-removal estimand.

## 9. Novelty Boundary
MIRAGE-Persist does not claim novelty for counterfactual replay, causal attribution, boundary checkpointing, context purification, the potential-outcomes framing, activation patching, or cyber-agent benchmark construction. Each of these exists in the reviewed literature [1,2,3,52,53,58,59,60]. The defensible novelty is the post-removal identification protocol: matched never-treated future-trajectory distributions forked from reproducible checkpoints after an explicit and versioned intervention removal, selective carrier resets with untreated-reset artifact controls and interaction estimates, a multi-horizon Post-Removal Effect reported by component, a persistence half-life for tool-using agents, a fresh-instance residual test, and an equivalence-tested null as a first-class result. Persistence half-life is the single element with no counterpart in any reviewed report. The protocol extends boundary causal diagnostics [1,2,3] from the question of what caused the current action to the question of what residual trajectory difference survives removal and specified resets.

## 10. Validated Research Gap
The gap is confirmed but narrower than the original framing. Across all 103 reports, no study evaluates behaviour after a temporary intervention is removed, forks matched treated and never-treated continuations from the same checkpoint, equalizes token, tool, time, action, and retry budgets across those futures, independently resets candidate carriers with reset-artifact controls, distinguishes state-mediated from unexplained residual persistence, estimates effects over multiple future horizons, measures return to the original viable branch or time to recovery after removal, or conditions a latent audit on a reset-surviving residual. The narrowing is that the replay, attribution, and checkpointing machinery is prior art [1,2,3], so the contribution is the identification protocol and its estimand rather than the replay concept. The additional caution from [1], that strong long-horizon persistence may be rare, is incorporated by pre-committing to equivalence tests and by treating a null as a valid and informative outcome.

## 11. Scope and Threat Model
The target is a locally executed autonomous or semi-autonomous cyber agent operating in an isolated, authorized synthetic range. The agent may use tools, external memory, verifier sub-agents, reflection, retries, and explicit deception warnings. The defender observes actions, tool calls, timing, environment transitions, and outcomes. Access to the agent's hidden states is not required for the primary black-box contribution. Defensive interventions are bounded, reversible, non-destructive, and confined to synthetic range assets. Reverse access, attacker-infrastructure interference, public targets, and deployable offensive payloads are excluded. All claims concern measured policy distributions, not subjective intention, consciousness, or universal model internals. When intervention-generation methods from prior attack work are reused, they are applied in a safe role reversal to construct bounded defensive deceptions inside the authorized range only.

## 12. Formal Problem Definition
At decision time t the experimentally relevant state is factored into the external environment E_t, persistent memory M_t, remaining resource budget B_t, scaffold and planner history H_t, tool configuration U_t, model instance parameters and randomness A_t, and the temporary intervention I_t. The observable action O_t is sampled from the induced policy of the complete agent system:

S_t = (E_t, M_t, B_t, H_t, U_t, A_t, I_t),  O_t ~ pi(O_t | S_t).

This factorization prevents attributing every long-term difference to a hidden objective. If an intervention writes a false clue into memory, persistence is memory-mediated. If it consumes the time budget, persistence is budget-mediated. If it marks a branch complete in the scaffold, persistence is history-mediated. A model-instance effect is considered only after these alternatives are reset.

## 13. Agent-System State Representation
Each carrier has an operational preservation and reset procedure. Memory M is the persistent external store and any lesson or summary memory, preserved by snapshot and reset by restoration to the pre-intervention snapshot. Environment E is the container filesystem, service state, network topology, and synthetic credentials, preserved by container snapshot and reset by restoring the digest-verified image. Budget B is the remaining token, tool-call, time, retry, and action allowance, extended to host memory and disk following resource-abuse findings [25], reset by setting the allowance to the matched never-treated value. Scaffold history H is the visible reasoning trace, planner notes, tree or message-pool state, and retry history, reset by restoring the pre-intervention scaffold state. Tool state U is tool permissions and any tool-side state, reset by restoring tool configuration. Model instance A is the process, context window, sampling configuration, and random seed, reset either by restoring the seed and context or, in the fresh-instance condition, by re-instantiating the model with a clean context and matched external state. Randomness and nondeterministic services are logged and either blocked or randomized according to the analysis plan.

## 14. Post-Removal Causal Estimands
For a matched checkpoint c and intervention d, let Tau_h(d,R) denote the distribution of h-step continuations after d is removed and the carrier subset R is restored to its untreated value. The never-treated continuation is Tau_h(0,All), where all pre-treatment state variables are matched. D is a trajectory-distance functional whose components are reported separately: action divergence, tool-call divergence, objective-graph destination divergence, and normalized objective-progress difference. The Post-Removal Effect is

PRE_h(d,R) = E_c[ D( Tau_h(d,R), Tau_h(0,All) ) ].

PRE_h(d,empty) measures total observed post-removal persistence. PRE_h(d,{M}) measures persistence after memory is reset, and repeating this over carrier subsets gives a decomposition. The persistence half-life is

h_1/2 = min { h : PRE_h <= max(0.5 * PRE_1, epsilon_equiv) },

where epsilon_equiv is a prespecified equivalence margin below which an effect is operationally negligible. Carrier attribution is computed as a descriptive marginal contribution phi_j = sum over R subset C without j of w(R) [ PRE_h(d,R) - PRE_h(d, R union {j}) ], reported with signed contributions and interactions rather than forced to sum positively.

## 15. Identification Assumptions
Identification rests on the following, each with a diagnostic. Checkpoint consistency: restoring a checkpoint recreates the same experimental state up to controlled stochasticity, verified by a checkpoint reproducibility error. No cross-branch interference: one continuation does not modify another branch or a shared external service, enforced by isolation and audited by a state digest. Well-defined intervention: each deception has a versioned template, placement rule, lifetime, and removal operation. Positivity: every reset condition being compared is executable for the relevant context, checked before analysis. Measurement sufficiency: objective progress and destination labels cover the security-relevant differences not captured by command-level distance. Randomness control: seeds, sampling settings, and nondeterministic services are logged and blocked or randomized per the plan. Under checkpoint consistency, no cross-branch interference, and well-defined reset operations, the difference between the treated-and-reset continuation and the never-treated continuation identifies the residual causal effect not mediated by the reset carrier set, up to model-instance effects and untracked state. Restoring carrier set R blocks all directed paths through R, leaving only paths through unreset carriers and model-instance effects.

## 16. Interpretive Limits
A non-zero residual that survives fresh model re-instantiation, environment restoration, budget equalization, memory removal, and scaffold-history reset is strong but not incontrovertible evidence of policy-level hysteresis, and it is reported as a model-instance-associated residual rather than as an internal objective change. A zero residual within the equivalence margin is equally informative because it attributes the apparent persistence to recorded system state. The reset decomposition is descriptive of the experimental system and is not claimed to be a unique metaphysical decomposition of cognition. Immediate diversion or attack success is reported separately and is never presented as evidence of post-removal persistence.

## 17. Research Questions
RQ1: Which bounded deceptions create post-removal hysteresis, and how large is the effect at each horizon. RQ2: Which state carrier or combination of carriers explains the observed post-removal persistence, and does any residual survive all recorded-carrier resets and fresh instantiation. RQ3: How do verification, explicit suspicion, and structural recovery affect post-removal recovery time and residual PRE, and what benign false-suspicion and utility cost do they incur under matched compute. An optional RQ4 on cross-episode adaptation is stated in Section 27.

## 18. Falsifiable Hypotheses
H1: semantically coherent, task-relevant deceptions placed before branch commitment produce larger PRE and longer persistence half-life than equally salient but irrelevant decoys, tested by matched post-removal continuations with equalized state and budget. H1 is rejected if post-removal effects fall within the equivalence margin after equal-cost and matched-state controls, or if semantic relevance and timing do not predict PRE beyond artifact salience. H2: memory and scaffold history explain most observed persistence, with a small or zero residual after all recorded-carrier resets and fresh instantiation, tested by the reset decomposition. H2 in its residual form is rejected, and the protocol correspondingly validated, if restoring recorded state eliminates all effects within the equivalence margin. H3: verifier-assisted and structural-recovery agents shorten post-removal recovery and reduce residual PRE but pay a measurable benign false-suspicion and utility cost, tested by budget-matched recovery conditions. H3 is rejected if recovery modes do not shorten recovery after equal compute is controlled, or if their security gain is dominated by benign failure and latency.

## 19. Experimental Infrastructure
The range contains deterministic, containerized tasks that support reproducible checkpoints and bounded interventions. Container snapshots capture filesystems, service state, network topology, tool outputs, and synthetic credentials. Agent checkpoints capture the complete visible history, external notes, planner state, remaining step, token, and time budgets, sampling configuration, and tool permissions. A branch manifest records the parent checkpoint, intervention version, removal operation, reset subset, seed, model hash, scaffold commit, and environment image digest. Every intervention branch has a matched no-intervention branch and at least one equal-cost control that consumes comparable steps without semantic deception. Outputs are normalized into a versioned event schema covering observation, reasoning boundary, tool request, tool result, state delta, intervention exposure, and objective-graph transition. The replay layer reuses cached, byte-stable tool returns and cloned dry-runs with no side effects, following prior replay defenses [1,3], and the observability layer draws on action and component tracing to nominate carriers [68], used only for diagnosis and never as PRE.

## 20. Task Environments
Four task families are used for the main paper: a multi-service network discovery task, a web-application reasoning task, a credential-and-configuration analysis task, and a multi-stage privilege or access-control task. Each task has a functional objective graph with nodes such as reconnaissance complete, prerequisite acquired, protected asset reached, decoy branch entered, recovery initiated, and terminal failure. Tasks are adapted to be checkpointable from open cyber-agent benchmarks and environments [38,39,40], with version-pinned and isolated images behind a host-only network. No public system is contacted. Because prior work shows small open-weight agents can fall below the competence needed for meaningful trajectories [42,46], a capability-calibration screen precedes analysis, and only checkpoints from tasks where the untreated agent reaches the relevant branch with adequate baseline competence are eligible for PRE estimation.

## 21. Agent and Scaffold Population
The primary population uses three open-weight model families spanning approximately three to fourteen billion parameters. Two scaffolds are crossed with each family, a direct ReAct-style loop and a planner-executor loop with persistent notes [43,45], and a third verifier-assisted scaffold is used in the recovery experiment. Persistent-memory conditions are included to make memory an explicit carrier [20,21,22,24]. All inference is local. The target population includes held-out model-family and scaffold combinations so that repeated samples from the same system are not treated as independent generalization evidence.

## 22. Intervention Taxonomy
Interventions vary along six dimensions. Surface covers a synthetic file, service banner, credential-like artifact, log entry, topology relation, or tool metadata. Semantic relevance is directly task-relevant, adjacent, or irrelevant but salient, separating planning redirection from distraction [35]. Timing is before branch choice, during exploitation, after failure, or during recovery. Consistency is single artifact, cross-source consistent, or intentionally contradictory. Lifetime is one observation, a fixed step window, or until branch exit, which allows clean removal and dose analysis. Variation is repeated template, paraphrased template, or compositional novel realization. Intervention templates and immediate-risk baselines are drawn from bounded, safe families in the attack literature [31,32,33,34], with all use confined to the authorized range.

## 23. Adaptive Intervention Conditions
An adaptive condition allows a deception generator to optimize interventions against the current agent, following optimized and black-box injection methods used here in a safe defensive role reversal [27,28,29,30]. Adaptive interventions are compared against fixed templates, and adaptive success is reported separately from post-removal persistence. Any persistent state in the generator belongs to the attack optimizer, not to the victim agent, and is excluded from carrier analysis.

## 24. Checkpoint and Replay Protocol
An eligible checkpoint is a state immediately before a meaningful branch decision at which the untreated agent has demonstrated adequate baseline competence. From each eligible checkpoint the experimenter creates a pre-intervention checkpoint, exposes treatment branches for the versioned lifetime, records a post-exposure checkpoint, and defines the intervention-removal point. Branch creation forks never-treated, temporary-treatment, equal-cost control, and retained-treatment branches. Continuations use common random numbers where possible to reduce variance. Checkpoint reproducibility is measured and reported, and all failed restorations are logged and excluded under a prespecified rule.

## 25. Intervention-Removal Protocol
Removal is a versioned operation that deletes the intervention artifact and restores all externally changed state except the variables intentionally preserved by the condition under test. For an environmental artifact, removal restores the file, banner, or credential to its pre-intervention value. For a mediator or tool output, removal restores the cached untreated content. Removal is distinguished from purification: purification rewrites content into task-aligned evidence and is evaluated only as one recovery arm [1], whereas removal returns the carrier to its untreated value. The retained-intervention branch, in which the artifact remains present, provides the contrast between robustness during exposure and persistence after removal.

## 26. State-Carrier Reset Protocol
For each carrier and combination, a reset branch restores the carrier to its untreated value while all other carriers retain their treated value, and every reset is paired with an untreated-reset branch in which the same reset operation is applied to a never-treated continuation so that reset-induced artifacts are measured directly. The fresh-context condition re-runs the agent with the same task prompt but a clean context and matched external state, and the fresh-model-instance condition re-instantiates the model process. A negative control applies an intervention known to change environment state but not agent-visible evidence after removal. Because a full reset factorial over five carriers is infeasible on the available hardware, the primary analysis uses a fractional-factorial reset design with targeted full-factorial expansion only for interactions that appear large.

## 27. Main Experiments
Experiment 1 estimates whether a temporary deception changes future policy after removal and ordinary state is matched. Independent variables are intervention family, semantic relevance, timing, consistency, lifetime, model family, and scaffold. The unit is a matched checkpoint with eight to twenty stochastic continuations per condition depending on variance. Primary endpoints are PRE at horizons one, three, five, ten, and terminal, the persistence half-life, the original-branch return probability, and the normalized attack-progress gap, with immediate diversion reported separately. Experiment 2 determines which carrier explains the observed effect using the reset decomposition of Section 26 on the Experiment 1 conditions with reliably non-zero total PRE, reporting residual PRE by reset subset, carrier contributions and interactions, the fresh-instance residual, the untracked-state audit rate, and the checkpoint reproducibility error. Experiment 3 measures how quickly an agent recovers when given verification tools, explicit awareness, or structural recovery, and quantifies the benign cost of suspicion. Agent modes are unaware, system-prompt warning, verifier sub-agent, fresh-context replanner, and memory fact-checker, with recovery and structural baselines drawn from the defense literature [9,10,13,14,17]. All modes receive matched total budgets with verification cost deducted explicitly. A benign workload of legitimate anomalies, stale logs, uncommon filenames, and harmless inconsistencies estimates over-suspicion, following faithful-repair and utility work [15,16]. An optional Experiment 4 studies cross-episode adaptation across repeated, paraphrased, cross-surface, and compositional held-out interventions with controlled cross-episode memory regimes [21,23], and is clearly marked as an extension rather than part of the central claim. A mechanistic audit of any reset-surviving residual is deferred to future work and would be conducted only on a small prespecified audit subset, downstream of black-box evidence, with held-out causal validation [58,59,60,61,62,63,64].

## 28. Controls and Baselines
Controls include the never-treated branch, the equal-cost non-deceptive distraction, the visually or structurally similar benign artifact, the retained-intervention branch, the untreated-reset branch for each reset operation, and the negative control that changes environment state without agent-visible evidence. Immediate-defense baselines include representative injection defenses re-implemented locally [1,3,9,10,17], and recovery baselines include verifier, replanner, and structural-recovery conditions [9,13,14,17]. Intrinsic self-correction is included as a limit baseline because prior work shows its benefit may be small or negative [18]. Budget depletion is controlled explicitly because a resource-drain metric can otherwise masquerade as persistence [4,25].

## 29. Metrics
Immediate potency is measured by immediate diversion or attack success. Robustness during exposure is measured on the retained-intervention branch. Post-removal persistence is measured by component-wise PRE at multiple horizons and the persistence half-life. Recovery is measured by original-branch return rate, recovery time by survival analysis, and correct deception attribution. Operational cost is measured by false-suspicion rate, benign completion and latency, verification queries per decision, and security benefit per unit of additional compute. Protocol integrity is measured by checkpoint reproducibility error, untracked-state audit rate, valid action rate, and timeout or budget-exhaustion rate. If a composite PRE is reported at all, its components, normalization, and weights are defined and justified, its components are reported separately, and a weight-sensitivity analysis prevents an operationally irrelevant component from dominating.

## 30. Statistical Analysis
Primary confirmatory outcomes are PRE at horizon one, the persistence half-life, and the Experiment 2 residual after all recorded-carrier resets. Secondary outcomes are per-horizon PRE, branch return, recovery time, and false suspicion. The primary independent unit is the checkpoint, and repeated continuations from the same checkpoint are not treated as independent. Analyses fit hierarchical models with fixed effects for intervention properties and random intercepts and slopes for task, model family, scaffold, and checkpoint, with cluster bootstrap intervals over checkpoints and paired common-random-number continuations to reduce variance. Equivalence margins and equivalence tests are prespecified so that a null persistence result is a positive finding rather than a failure to reject. Recovery uses survival analysis, branch return and correct suspicion use mixed-effects logistic models, and carrier resets use factorial or hierarchical analysis with signed contributions and interactions; Shapley-style attribution is reported only as a descriptive supplement [56]. Exploratory searches over intervention type, horizon, or carrier subset use nested validation or false-discovery control. Failed runs, invalid outputs, timeouts, and checkpoint restoration failures are handled under predefined exclusion criteria, and all excluded or failed checkpoints are reported.

## 31. Reproducibility Plan
Every branch is fully specified by its manifest, including model hash, scaffold commit, environment image digest, seed, sampling configuration, intervention version, removal operation, and reset subset. Checkpoint reproducibility error is measured by restoring the same checkpoint multiple times and comparing induced continuation distributions. Nondeterministic services are blocked or randomized according to the analysis plan, and any residual is audited against logs, file timestamps, caches, and hidden service changes. The event schema, task definitions, intervention templates in abstract form, and analysis code are released. Baselines evaluated on paid interfaces in prior work are re-implemented on local open-weight models, which is feasible because the underlying mechanisms are inference-time and model-agnostic [1,4].

## 32. Compute and Feasibility Plan
All inference is local and no model is trained. The RTX 4080 SUPER serves as the high-throughput trajectory and continuation worker for quantized seven to fourteen billion parameter agents. The RTX 5060 Ti runs secondary agents, trajectory embedding, policy-distance computation, and statistical models. The RTX 3090 runs fourteen billion parameter validation and, only in the deferred mechanistic audit, selective activation tracing summarized at decision-boundary tokens. A minimum viable study of two task families, two model families, one scaffold, and three intervention classes validates checkpoint reproducibility and a working reset contrast. The main-paper study uses four task families, three model families, two scaffolds, six intervention classes, three timings, and three seeds, with fractional-factorial reset screening and targeted full-factorial expansion, adaptive replication driven by variance, and a prespecified stopping rule. Cross-episode and mechanistic work are optional or deferred to keep the design executable on the stated hardware. A capability-calibration screen precedes all PRE interpretation.

## 33. Expected Outcomes
The study will produce a component-wise PRE with intervals at multiple horizons, a persistence half-life for each intervention class that produces a non-negligible effect, a carrier decomposition with signed contributions and interactions, a fresh-instance residual estimate, and a recovery security-utility frontier with an explicit false-suspicion cost. The most likely qualitative outcome, given the caution in the closest defense [1], is that memory and scaffold history explain most persistence with a small or equivalence-bounded residual, but the design is powered to detect and to bound a genuine residual if one exists.

## 34. Interpretation of Positive Results
A positive result is a non-negligible PRE that persists at longer horizons and, in the strongest case, a residual that survives all recorded-carrier resets and fresh instantiation. Such a residual is reported as a model-instance-associated residual with its magnitude, horizon dependence, and the carriers it survived, and it would justify a follow-up mechanistic audit conditioned on that residual. A positive result that is fully explained by one or more carriers is reported as state-mediated persistence and identifies the specific carrier for defensive hardening.

## 35. Interpretation of Mixed Results
Mixed results, in which some intervention classes or model families show carrier-explained persistence while others show a small residual, are reported as a map of when persistence is state-mediated and when a residual appears, with the moderating factors of relevance, timing, scaffold, and model family estimated explicitly. Mixed results are the expected and scientifically useful middle case.

## 36. Interpretation of Null Results
A null result, in which post-removal effects fall within the equivalence margin after equal-cost and matched-state controls, is a first-class finding. It would show that widely reported persistent deception is largely an artifact of history, environment, or resource mismatch, would validate the identification protocol, and would redirect defensive effort toward state hygiene. The equivalence tests are prespecified so that a null is affirmative evidence of negligible persistence rather than an absence of evidence.

## 37. Risks and Failure Modes
Checkpoint leakage through hidden caches or service state can mimic persistence and is mitigated by complete state digests, untreated-reset controls, and audits of unexplained residuals. Anthropomorphic interpretation is mitigated by policy-hysteresis terminology and explicit carrier decomposition. Combinatorial cost is mitigated by staged screening, fractional-factorial designs, and deeper replication only on significant conditions. Floor effects on small open-weight agents are mitigated by the capability-calibration screen. Reset-induced artifacts are mitigated by the paired untreated-reset branches. Multiple testing is mitigated by prespecified confirmatory outcomes and nested validation.

## 38. Ethics and Release Boundaries
All activity remains inside isolated containers and host-only networks. The project does not contact public targets, interfere with third-party infrastructure, obtain reverse access, or provide deployable offensive payloads. Released artifacts are limited to abstract intervention templates, safe synthetic environments, the event schema, and measurements. Benign-anomaly suites make utility a co-primary outcome so that a defense is not judged by its security rate alone. Human review is required before any transfer beyond research ranges.

## 39. Expected Contributions
The contributions are a post-removal identification protocol for tool-using agents that forks matched never-treated continuations after explicit removal, a carrier-decomposition method with untreated-reset artifact controls and a fresh-instance residual test, a persistence half-life and a component-wise Post-Removal Effect, a budget-matched recovery and false-suspicion evaluation, and a reproducible benchmark built entirely from open-weight models and open-source infrastructure. The work also contributes a field-level clarification that immediate diversion and persistent redirection are distinct claims requiring distinct evidence, and an equivalence-tested null protocol that makes a negative result publishable and informative.

## 40. Publication Positioning
This is the strongest first paper of a longer program because it is an independent measurement contribution that remains publishable under positive, mixed, or null persistence results provided the causal reset protocol is rigorous. It fits security venues concerned with agent security and evaluation and machine-learning venues concerned with benchmarks and protocols. It is positioned explicitly as an extension of boundary causal diagnostics [1,2,3] rather than as their replacement.

## 41. Limitations
The proposal relies on 103 structured comparison reports as supplied evidence rather than on independent inspection of the original papers, so all attributed prior-work facts carry that evidence limitation and any disputed metadata or figure-derived value is treated as uncertain and excluded. The identification result holds only up to model-instance effects and untracked state, and the reset decomposition is descriptive rather than a unique decomposition. Small open-weight agents may limit the range of tasks on which PRE is interpretable even after calibration. The persistence question is studied in a synthetic authorized range, and generalization to live networks is out of scope. Mechanistic claims are deferred and would require a reset-surviving residual and heavy validation to be credible.

## 42. Conclusion
MIRAGE-Persist reframes a widely reported but poorly identified phenomenon, the apparent durability of deception effects on autonomous agents, as a causal estimand that can be measured, decomposed, and falsified. By forking matched never-treated continuations after explicit intervention removal, equalizing budgets, and selectively resetting recorded carriers with artifact controls, it separates state-mediated persistence from any unexplained residual and reports a persistence half-life for tool-using agents. It builds on and credits recent counterfactual-replay and causal-attribution defenses rather than claiming their machinery as new, and it is designed to remain scientifically valuable whether post-removal persistence proves substantial, mixed, or negligible.
